## Interpretation
1. **E4 — "fewer, larger, better-timed" is exactly what the data show.** Under
   CA's 55% bar, school districts propose **less than half as often** as TX
   districts under 50% (8.8 vs 20.1 per 100 districts/yr), at **2.6× the median
   size** ($39M vs $15M), and **4.4× more on-cycle** (62.7% vs 14.1% — timing to
   the high-turnout electorate that clears a supermajority). WI sits between on
   all three. The agenda margin is doing heavy, visible work. (MN shows zero
   because its question-text purpose classification misses school bonds — a
   classification gap, flagged, not evidence.)
2. **E2 — bunching is concentrated in TX (ratio 1.55, z=3.42)**, mild in CA
   schools (1.18, n.s.). Caveat: TX's frame includes tiny-electorate special
   districts (MUD votes with double-digit turnout), where lumpy vote shares
   mechanically produce near-cutoff mass — engineering and discreteness are
   confounded there; the CA pattern is the cleaner engineering read.
3. **E3 — the agenda absorbs a 55% bar but not a two-thirds bar.** Pass rates:
   TX (50) 79.2% ≈ CA schools (55) 77.6% ≈ LA/NC/MN (50) 83.3% — near-invariant
   despite the higher hurdle, exactly as engineered proposals imply. Only the
   66.7% regime breaks the invariance (47.6%): moderate bars are neutralized at
   the agenda margin; extreme bars bind even what gets proposed.
4. **TX-2019 — bundling is causally rule-driven.** At the separate-proposition
   mandate (eff. late 2019), propositions per election jump 1.5–1.7 → 2.0–2.4
   and the multi-proposition share doubles (≈25% → 49–60%) precisely at the rule
   change — an in-panel, sharply-timed unbundling. (2019 reporting-mandate
   coverage expansion noted; the within-election count is robust to it.)
