## Interpretation — the licence, and where the shortfalls actually come from
1. **Extraction reads real events.** Where an OS carries an election_date, it
   matches a referendum we independently observe for that unit **67.9%** of the
   time pooled — and **95.4% in WI**, where the DPI registry is the most complete.
   Conditional on a match, **91.3%** point to a *passed* measure (98.0% in CA,
   98.8% in NC): the extractor is citing genuine, won elections.
2. **Non-matches trace to registry windows, not extraction error.** MN matches
   only 19.6% — its registry covers 2020–25 while OS-cited elections reach
   earlier; MA's Prop 2½ ledger dates votes at month grain. The match rate rises
   exactly where the registry is known complete.
3. **T2 shortfall is authorization spend-down, mostly.** 76.2% of voter-mode
   new-money docs have a passed referendum ≤6y in our records (WI 90.5%). TX at
   76.8% despite a near-complete BRB registry shows the cause: districts draw on
   authorizations older than 6y (bonds issued in series for a decade). The
   support rate is a conservative lower bound, not an error rate.
4. **T3 is consistent with real dual-channel behavior.** 78.3% of TX
   council-mode docs lack a recent passed GO vote; the 21.7% that have one are
   largely genuine concurrent channels (TX certificates of obligation are
   council-authorized and coexist with voter GO programs) — weak-evidence test,
   as designed.
**Licence:** in states with complete registries the OS authorization fields track
the election record at ~90%+; discrepancies concentrate precisely where registry
coverage is known-short. Deploying `auth_mode_final2` nationally (C1/C2) is
measurement-justified; cite this table.
