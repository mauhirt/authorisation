## Interpretation
1. **The excess density above the cutoff is real** (θ=+0.244, z=4.55 at h=5:
   ~24% more mass just above than just below). This does **not** invalidate the
   design: precise manipulation of a public vote count is infeasible, and the
   pattern is the signature of **endogenous proposal timing** — governments
   propose when they expect to (narrowly) win — documented for school bonds since
   Cellini–Ferreira–Rothstein (2010, QJE), who proceed with the same design. In
   this paper's language the density asymmetry is itself evidence on the **agenda
   margin (H1a)**: the coalition requirement disciplines what gets proposed.
2. **Identification survives it.** The donut-RD — dropping the |margin|≤0.5/1/2pp
   region where any sorting would concentrate — leaves the effect stable or
   larger (GO issuance τ +0.19 to +0.20, z 3.7–5.3, vs +0.146 with no donut),
   and the ±5pp covariate balance table shows 0/7 imbalances. Whatever puts
   extra measures just above the cutoff is not generating the issuance jump.
3. **Paper treatment:** report the McCrary θ openly; present donut-RD as the
   headline robustness; frame the density asymmetry as H1a evidence, not a nuisance.
